\section{Results}

Results are scored over the thesis's observation window (periods 2--11), with full-horizon values (periods 1--15) as a sensitivity; records and summaries are in the supplement (pages 02--10 of \path{supplementary/}).

\subsection{Reproduction of scenarios}\label{reproduction-of-scenarios}

\textbf{Inconsistency is frequent under harvest-flow constraints.} Over periods 2--11, 49\% of the flow-constrained scenarios (178/360) are inconsistent by the divergence metric (equation~\eqref{eq:metric}), 33\% (88/270) at positive rates, against 47\% of the flow-unconstrained (\scen{NHF}) control (34/72), whose divergence is confined to 0\% (18/18) and 2\% (16/18). Over all fifteen periods, the flow-constrained share is 56\%. Occurrence depends on the tolerance (68\%, 49\%, and 26\% at 2\%, 5\%, and 10\%), but the contrast between replanning institutions below holds at every tolerance in this range.

\textbf{The reproduction finds smaller magnitudes than the thesis.} On the thesis's own measure (equation~5-1) and the thesis's factor combinations that we can run, mean volume inconsistency is 7.8\% on the landbases with over-mature stands (1--6), 3.4\% on young-growth landbases 7--10, and 3.7\% on the random landbases, against 11.9\%, 6.8\%, and 5.3\% in the thesis \citep[Table~6.2]{daugherty1991credibility}, whose subsets also include runs with restricted management choices. Over-mature landbases are the most inconsistent in both studies, but we do not separate the young-growth and random groups, which the thesis found significantly different \citep[p.~100]{daugherty1991credibility}. Two of the thesis's patterns are not reproduced. Its highest inconsistency was on landbases 5 and 6 (19\% and 24\%; \citealp[pp.~118, 125]{daugherty1991credibility}; here 10.2\% and 6.4\%, with landbase 3 highest at 11.3\%). Its inconsistency also rose when non-declining yield was replaced by any of the other four policies \citep[p.~111]{daugherty1991credibility}; at 4\%, it was 6.0\% under non-declining yield and 7.1--16.1\% under the others \citep[Table~6.8]{daugherty1991credibility}. Here the ordering is largely reversed: 5.4\% under non-declining yield, 2.4--3.6\% under three of the others, and 6.3\% only under symmetric bounded deviation of 20\%. The thesis's per-landbase classification of its reference run \citep[p.~124]{daugherty1991credibility} is matched on 6 of 18 landbases (details in \path{supplementary/10-review-analyses.md}).

\textbf{The declining non-declining yield.} On landbase~1 at 4\%, the open-loop non-declining-yield plan projects \num{252400}~m\(^3\) per period (Figure~\ref{fig:ndy}). Under replanning, the realized harvest falls below this level in every later period, declining steadily to \num{210600}~m\(^3\) by period 11; over periods 2--11 it is 9.5\% below the announced total (12.1\% at 2\%, 18.8\% at 0\%). On landbase~2 it is below the announced level in all 14 later periods at every rate. This is the ``declining non-declining yield'' \citep[p.~332]{gunn2007models}.

\textbf{Occurrence at every discount rate.} Flow-constrained occurrence is 100\% at 0\% and 29\%, 31\%, and 38\% at 2\%, 4\%, and 6\%, consistent with the thesis's result that a positive rate is not required; mean magnitude is 0.16 at 0\% and 0.04--0.05 at 2--6\% (Table~\ref{tab:grid}).

\textbf{The divergence is not an artifact of alternate optima, but its first step is often small.} On landbase~1 under \scen{NDY}, the objective-gap diagnostic finds the announced plan strictly suboptimal or infeasible in 9 or 10 of the ten replans of periods 2--11 at every rate, whereas the flow-unconstrained control's plan stays optimal at 2--6\% (and in 9 of 10 replans at 0\%). Across scenarios, this holds in 58\% of flow-constrained and 25\% of control replans (the latter only at 0--2\%), and every inconsistent scenario has such a replan. The first deviation is often small: when the announced plan is suboptimal rather than infeasible, the gap is a median 0.05\% of the optimal objective (90th percentile 0.95\%). The diagnostic also flags 146 of the 182 flow-constrained scenarios below the divergence tolerance: it detects departures, the metric measures their size.

\textbf{Effect of the harvest-flow policy.} Non-declining yield is the most frequently inconsistent policy (50/72, 69\%; 86\% over 1--15), followed by symmetric bounded deviation of 20\% (56\%); bounded decline is the least (36\% and 39\% at 10\% and 20\%; Table~\ref{tab:grid}).

\textbf{The replanning institution determines whether the plan is followed.} Flow-constrained occurrence falls from 49\% in the core design (rolling horizon, reset flow history) to 27\% with a fixed horizon, 24\% with carried flow history, and 1 of 360 scenarios with both, the exact tail problem, under which 99.9\% of replans find the announced period-1 harvest optimal, as Bellman's principle requires (Table~\ref{tab:institutions}). Ties among stand-level alternatives of equal value can still send the realized forest along another path: in 5 of the 360 scenarios (4 within periods 2--11) the realized harvest departs from the plan by more than 1\% in some period. With carried history and a rolling horizon, the remaining inconsistency is almost all at 0\% (6 of 270 scenarios at positive rates). The control's low-rate divergence is a rolling-horizon effect: it vanishes with a fixed horizon. Carried history with a rolling horizon corresponds to the thesis's test on landbases 1--10 at 4\%, which reduced volume inconsistency to 1.9\% \citep[pp.~118--120]{daugherty1991credibility}; on the same scenarios we obtain 0.55\% (5.2\% with reset history).

\textbf{Role of the initial forest.} Inconsistency occurs on all eighteen landbases (occurrence 35--100\%); magnitude is 0.07--0.15 on landbases 1--6, 0.04--0.08 on 7--10, and about 0.05 on 11--18. The negatively valued CM-CE ecoclass, the thesis's \emph{inconsistent variables} that can enter a plan only as filler relaxing a binding flow constraint, raises inconsistency: landbases with it are more often inconsistent than their pairs without it (67/80 vs 37/80), with volume inconsistency 4.7 percentage points higher on average (higher in 84\% of the 80 pairs), against 3 points in the thesis's 58 pairs \citep[p.~116]{daugherty1991credibility}. Four further draws of the random landbases give occurrence of 38--41\% (reported draw 36\%) and magnitude about 0.05.

\subsection{Extension results for mitigation tests}

\textbf{E1: Declining discount rates, re-applied by each planner, mostly increase inconsistency.} Flow-constrained occurrence is 99--100\% under the inverse-j paths and 61\% under the linear 4\%-to-0\% path, against 31\% at a constant 4\%, but 39\% under the linear 6\%-to-0\% path, as at a constant 6\% (38\%); magnitude is 0.05--0.23 (Fig.~\ref{fig:e1}, Table~\ref{tab:extensions}). The flow-unconstrained control diverges in all 18 scenarios under every path. This is expected for planners who each re-apply a declining schedule, but is not separated here from the low-rate rolling-horizon effect.

\textbf{E2: A calibrated harvest cap nearly removes measured inconsistency.} All 72 scenarios converge, and 2 are inconsistent, both at 0\% (mean magnitude 0.004 over periods 2--11); a single period deviates by more than 5\% in 5 scenarios (at most 13.6\%). This is largely by construction: in periods 2--11, the cap binds in 99\% of planned and 94\% of realized periods, where announced and realized harvests coincide, and 96\% of replans find the announced plan optimal. On landbase~1 at 4\%, the cap is \num{225100}~m\(^3\) per period, 11\% below the \scen{NDY} plan's announced level (Fig.~\ref{fig:e2}). We compare the cap with replanned \scen{NDY} over periods 1--11, excluding the original horizon's end periods, where only \scen{NDY} is terminal-constrained: on landbase~1 at 4\%, the cap's realized harvest is 2.3\% lower and its NPV 6.3\% lower; across scenarios, the differences range from $-15$\% to $+35$\% in volume and $-8$\% to $+44$\% in NPV (medians $+1.4$\% and $-0.5$\%).

\textbf{E3: A revenue-denominated flow does not mitigate inconsistency overall.} Occurrence is 48\% (171/360, vs 49\% volume-denominated; also 171/360 when scored on revenue), with slightly larger magnitude (0.08 vs 0.07). It halves under \scen{NDY} (69\% to 35\%) but rises under symmetric bounded deviation of 20\% (56\% to 82\%), with little change under the other policies. The revenue floor does not keep CM-CE out of the plan (on landbase~1 at 4\%, 2.2\% of the projected revenue-\scen{NDY} harvest; 5.3\% under volume \scen{NDY}), and the paired landbases with and without CM-CE remain apart (50/80 vs 45/80). The \scen{NDY} result is partly a weaker constraint: a non-declining revenue floor lets volume fall through escalating net values and shifts toward more valuable ecoclasses; on landbase~1 at 4\%, the revenue-\scen{NDY} plan harvests 82\% more than volume \scen{NDY} in period 1, then 38\% less in period 2 than in period 1 (single-period falls reach 70\% elsewhere). In McQuillan's small example, removing the negatively valued stand had restored consistency \citep[p.~25]{daugherty1991credibility}.

\textbf{E4: The anchoring of the flow constraint matters, its shape does not.} With within-plan windows, rolling-mean \scen{NDY} behaves like pointwise \scen{NDY} (occurrence 69\% in both). With realized-history windows, occurrence falls to 27\% (33 of 39 inconsistent scenarios at 0\%) and magnitude to 0.05, but the floor's realized-history part must be loosened in 65\% of replans (72--74\% at 2--6\%), by a median 1.3\% (at most 2.3\%), so the failure to maintain non-declining yield appears as small, recorded shortfalls. This mirrors carrying the flow history (Table~\ref{tab:institutions}).
