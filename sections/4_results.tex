\section{Results}

We report summarized results of the full scenario grid (18~landbases $\times$ 4~discount rates $\times$ 6~harvest-flow policies, i.e., 432~sequential-replanning simulations). These results reproduce and refine \citet{daugherty1991credibility}'s findings on the real Umpqua FORPLAN case study. Afterwards, we report the results of the extension experiments aimed at mitigating dynamic inconsistencies. The full per-period results are available in the project repository (\texttt{results/experiments/}), summarized with figures and tables in the supplement (\texttt{supplementary/02-core-grid.md} and \texttt{supplementary/05}-\texttt{08}).

\subsection{Reproduction of scenarios}

\textbf{The harvest-flow constraint is the operative link.} Dynamic inconsistency is pervasive under the harvest-flow policies but largely absent without one. Across the scenario grid, 73\% of the flow-constrained scenarios (NDY, bounded decline, and bounded deviation) exhibit inconsistency by the divergence metric (equation~\eqref{eq:metric}), against 38\% of the unconstrained harvest-flow (NHF) control. The NHF divergence is concentrated at the lowest discount rates (0--2\%), where the objective is nearly indifferent to timing. At 4--6\% the unconstrained control is consistent. In all flow-constrained scenarios, the first-period decision is consistent. The divergence occurs in the plan's tail, exactly as the theory predicts. Where a model links periods through a harvest-flow constraint, its projected harvest trajectory is a property of a plan that sequential re-optimization might not follow.

\textbf{The declining non-declining yield.} On the all-mature landbase~1, we found a remarkable divergence between announced and realized harvests (Figure~\ref{fig:ndy}). The open-loop non-declining-yield plan projects a smooth, credible-looking level harvest flow; the realized replanned trajectory is revised downward over most of the horizon as each successive planner inherits the depleted forest and re-solves. This is the ``declining non-declining yield'' of \citet[pp.~331--332]{gunn2007models}: the promised non-declining flow declines precisely because each successive planner declines to honour it.

\textbf{Occurrence at every discount rate; magnitude is rate-dependent.} Inconsistency occurs at every discount rate in the scenario grid (the flow-constrained occurrence rate is 100\% at 0\% and 47--86\% at 2--6\%), consistent with the thesis's analytical result. As the discount factor is uniform across periods, it drops out of the consistency requirements \citep[ch.~3, p.~58--59]{daugherty1991credibility}. The \emph{magnitude} of the deviation between announces and realized harvest volumes is rate-dependent: on landbase~1 under NDY, the mean relative divergence and the total-volume shortfall are markedly larger at a 0\% rate (where the objective is indifferent to timing) than at 2--6\%. We return to this nuance in the Discussion, because it refines, rather than contradicts, the naive account of the phenomenon.

\textbf{The divergence is not an artifact of alternate optima.} Because LP optima can be non-unique, a divergence between the projected and realized trajectories could in principle reflect a different member of an optimal set rather than a plan that will not be followed. The objective-gap diagnostic (Section~3.3) rules this out: it re-solves each subproblem freely and again with the period-1 harvest held to the announced plan's value, and compares the objectives. On landbase~1 under NDY, the announced plan's tail is strictly suboptimal or outright infeasible from the realized state in 13--14 of 15 replan periods at every discount rate -- the re-solving planner strictly improves on, or cannot even implement, the announced tail. Under the unconstrained control, by contrast, the announced tail remains optimal at every period at 4--6\% (and largely optimal at 0\%, where the flat objective admits alternate optima). The inconsistency is therefore a property of the flow-constrained plan rather than being attributable to non-unique LP optima.

\textbf{Effect of the harvest-flow policy.} The strictness and form of the flow constraint shape the inconsistency. The non-declining-yield policy produces the most frequent divergence (occurrence 86\%, the highest of the flow policies), and the magnitude of the divergence varies by policy and landbase (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). At the base and higher discount rates (4--6\%) the unconstrained control is consistent (0\% occurrence), confirming the flow constraint as the operative between-period link. At the lowest rates (0--2\%), the control also diverges, which we attribute to timing-indifference (a near-flat objective admits near-tie optima that the re-solver resolves differently) rather than genuine inconsistency. The objective-gap diagnostic confirms that the control's tail remains optimal there.

\textbf{Role of disequilibrium age-class structure.} The inconsistency occurs across all eighteen initial forest conditions (flow-constrained occurrence 50--100\% by landbase), and is most pronounced in magnitude in the disequilibrium structures: the all-mature and area-control-derived landbases, which place a pulse of immediately harvestable, financially over-mature volume in front of a regulated future that cannot sustain it. The CM-CE ecoclass is negatively valued, its harvest cost exceeds its timber value, and such strata enter the open-loop solutions as \emph{inconsistent variables}: area allocations whose only function is to relax the binding flow constraint in the plan and that the re-solving planner subsequently abandons.

\subsection{Extension results for mitigation tests}

\textbf{E1: Declining discount rates increase inconsistencies.} Replacing the constant rate with declining schedules (linear or inverse-j) raises flow-constrained occurrence to 98--100\% (vs.\ 47--86\% at constant 2--6\%) and mean magnitude to 0.16--0.20 (vs.\ 0.07--0.11). Weighting the far future more heavily does not mitigate the inconsistencies (Fig.~\ref{fig:e1}, Table~\ref{tab:extensions}). Even under the unconstrained flow control inconsistency occurs.  While the plan under constant 4--6\% rates was consistent (occurrence 0\%), under every declining path, it diverges at 100\% occurrence, and the objective-gap diagnostic shows the announced tails to be suboptimal or infeasible (strictly suboptimal in 30--61\% of tail periods; infeasible in 7--14\%). With no inter-period constraint present, the inconsistency is driven by a different mechanism. Each future planner re-applies the declining path from her own present and is therefore more impatient about her near term than the time-zero planner was about the same calendar period. Declining-rate schedules thus not only fail to mitigate the structural inconsistency, but they also add a second, independent mechanism that operates even where the structural one is absent.

\textbf{E2: A calibrated max-harvest constraint eliminates inconsistencies.} Under the iterative maximum-yield-search policy, the inconsistency disappears; all 72~cells converge, and the occurrence is 0\% at every discount rate (mean divergence 0.004, grid maximum 0.025 -- all below the 5\% tolerance). The objective-gap diagnostic confirms genuine consistency rather than tie-breaking: 96\% of replan periods leave the announced tail optimal (residual suboptimal/infeasible shares 3\%/0.2\%, noise level). On landbase~1 at 4\%, the calibrated even-flow level is about 9,400~MCF/period and roughly 8\% below the NDY plan's announced 10,250 MCF/period that sequential replanning will not deliver (Fig.~\ref{fig:e2}). The  max-harvest constraint reveals the costs of the credibility of the harvest-flow promise. The gap between the announced NDY level and the calibrated level quantifies the allowable-cut effect.

\textbf{E3: Denominating the flow in dollars increases inconsistencies.} Under revenue-denominated flow policies, flow-constrained occurrence rises to 99\% (vs.\ 73\% volume-denominated) and mean magnitude roughly doubles (0.16 vs. 0.12). Under volume NDY the open-loop plan schedules negatively valued CM-CE harvest equal to 2.5--3.5\% of projected volume on landbase~1 (the inconsistent-variable \textit{filler}). Under revenue NDY the projected harvest of negatively valued strata is exactly zero, the filler is not applied. Yet the plan is still not followed. In contrast, the revenue-NDY plan front-loads harvests more strongly, with the period-1 realized harvest being about 30\% above the volume-NDY case. The negatively valued strata are therefore observable markers of possible inconsistencies, not their driver. The driver of inconsistencies is the flow link in combination with the disequilibrium state. It also operates through the positive-value strata alone when the filler is unavailable.

\textbf{E4: The divergence is robust to alternative implementations of non-declining yield but sensitive to how the constraint is anchored.} Under the within-plan institution, rolling-mean NDY is indistinguishable from pointwise NDY (occurrence 89\% vs.\ 86\%; magnitude 0.10).. Under the realized-history institution, occurrence falls to 69\% and magnitude to 0.072: binding the future planner to what actually happened mitigates the divergence. But the floor then frequently cannot be sustained from the depleted realized state. In 21\% of replan periods (up to 32\% at 6\% interest rate), the policy must be relaxed, such that the failure to maintain non-declining yield is recorded as infeasibility rather than occurring through implicit abandonment of the previously announced trajectory. Both approaches present plausible models of planning practice. Their contrasting outcomes show that the replanning institution is itself consequential, rather than merely a robustness consideration in the core design.
