\section{Results}

We report summarized results of the full scenario grid (18~landbases $\times$ 4~discount rates $\times$ 6~harvest-flow policies, i.e., 432~sequential-replanning simulations). These results reproduce and refine the findings of \citet{daugherty1991credibility} on the case study built from the Umpqua National Forest planning data. Afterwards, we report the results of the extension experiments aimed at mitigating dynamic inconsistencies. The full per-period results are available in the project repository (\path{results/experiments/}), summarized with figures and tables in the supplement (\path{supplementary/02-core-grid.md} and \path{supplementary/05}-\texttt{08}).

\subsection{Reproduction of scenarios}

\textbf{The harvest-flow constraint is the operative link.} Dynamic inconsistency is pervasive under the harvest-flow policies but largely absent without one. Across the scenario grid, 73\% of the flow-constrained scenarios (263/360; \scen{NDY}, bounded decline, and bounded deviation) exhibit inconsistency by the divergence metric (equation~\eqref{eq:metric}), against 38\% of the unconstrained harvest-flow (\scen{NHF}) control (27/72). The \scen{NHF} divergence is concentrated at the lowest discount rates (0--2\%), where the objective is nearly indifferent to timing. At 4--6\% the unconstrained control is consistent. The divergence occurs in the plan's tail, exactly as the theory predicts. Where a model links periods through a harvest-flow constraint, its projected harvest trajectory is a property of a plan that sequential re-optimization might not follow.

\textbf{The declining non-declining yield.} On the all-mature landbase~1, we found a remarkable divergence between announced and realized harvests (Figure~\ref{fig:ndy}). The open-loop non-declining-yield plan projects a smooth, credible-looking level harvest flow; the realized replanned trajectory is revised downward over most of the horizon as each successive planner inherits the depleted forest and re-solves. This is the ``declining non-declining yield'' of \citet[pp.~331--332]{gunn2007models}: the promised non-declining flow declines precisely because each successive planner declines to honor it.

\textbf{Occurrence at every discount rate; magnitude is rate-dependent.} Inconsistency occurs at every discount rate in the scenario grid (the flow-constrained occurrence rate is 100\% at 0\% (90/90) and 47--86\% at 2--6\% (42/90 to 77/90)), consistent with the thesis's result that a positive rate is not required for inconsistency to occur. The \emph{magnitude} of the deviation between announced and realized harvest volumes is rate-dependent: on landbase~1 under \scen{NDY}, the mean relative divergence and the total-volume shortfall are markedly larger at a 0\% rate (where the objective is indifferent to timing) than at 2--6\%. We return to this nuance in the Discussion, because it refines, rather than contradicts, the naive account of the phenomenon.

\textbf{The divergence is not an artifact of alternate optima.} The objective-gap diagnostic (Section~\ref{the-sequential-replanning-simulator}) shows that, on landbase~1 under \scen{NDY}, the announced plan's tail is strictly suboptimal or infeasible from the realized state in 13--14 of 15 replan periods at every discount rate, whereas under the unconstrained control it remains optimal at every period at 4--6\% (and largely optimal at 0\%, where the flat objective admits alternate optima). The inconsistency is therefore a property of the flow-constrained plan, not of non-unique LP optima.

\textbf{Effect of the harvest-flow policy.} The strictness and form of the flow constraint shape the inconsistency. The non-declining-yield policy produces the most frequent divergence (occurrence 86\%, the highest of the flow policies), and the magnitude of the divergence varies by policy and landbase (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). At the base and higher discount rates (4--6\%) the unconstrained control is consistent (0\% occurrence), confirming the flow constraint as the operative between-period link. At the lowest rates (0--2\%), the control also diverges, which we attribute to timing-indifference (a near-flat objective admits near-tie optima that the re-solver resolves differently) rather than genuine inconsistency. The objective-gap diagnostic confirms that the control's tail remains optimal there.

\textbf{Role of disequilibrium age-class structure.} The inconsistency occurs across all eighteen initial forest conditions (flow-constrained occurrence 50--100\% by landbase), and is most pronounced in magnitude in the disequilibrium structures: the all-mature and area-control-derived landbases, which place a pulse of immediately harvestable, financially over-mature volume in front of a regulated future that cannot sustain it. The CM-CE ecoclass is negatively valued, its harvest cost exceeds its timber value, and such strata enter the open-loop solutions as \emph{inconsistent variables}: area allocations whose only function is to relax the binding flow constraint in the plan and that the re-solving planner subsequently abandons.

\subsection{Extension results for mitigation tests}

\textbf{E1: Declining discount rates increase inconsistencies.} Replacing the constant rate with declining schedules (linear or inverse-j) raises flow-constrained occurrence to 98--100\% (vs.\ 47--86\% at constant 2--6\%) and mean magnitude to 0.16--0.20 (vs.\ 0.07--0.11; Fig.~\ref{fig:e1}, Table~\ref{tab:extensions}). Declining rates also make the control without a harvest-flow constraint (\scen{NHF}) inconsistent: consistent under constant 4--6\% rates (occurrence 0\%), it diverges under every declining path (occurrence 100\%), with announced tails strictly suboptimal in 30--61\% of tail periods and infeasible in 7--14\%. With no inter-period constraint present, this second inconsistency arises from the declining schedule itself (Section~\ref{the-discount-rate-nuance}).

\textbf{E2: A calibrated max-harvest constraint eliminates inconsistencies.} Under the iterative maximum-yield-search policy, the inconsistency disappears; all 72~cells converge, and the occurrence is 0\% at every discount rate (mean divergence 0.001, grid maximum 0.008---all below the 5\% tolerance). The objective-gap diagnostic confirms genuine consistency rather than tie-breaking: 96\% of replan periods leave the announced tail optimal (residual suboptimal/infeasible shares 3.5\%/0.2\%, noise level). On landbase~1 at 4\%, the calibrated even-flow level is about 9,400~MCF/period and roughly 8\% below the \scen{NDY} plan's announced 10,250 MCF/period that sequential replanning will not deliver (Fig.~\ref{fig:e2}). The  max-harvest constraint reveals the costs of the credibility of the harvest-flow promise. The gap between the announced \scen{NDY} level and the calibrated level quantifies the allowable-cut effect.

\textbf{E3: Denominating the flow in dollars mitigates inconsistencies.} Under revenue-denominated flow policies, flow-constrained occurrence falls to 56\% (200/360, vs.\ 73\%, 263/360, volume-denominated) and mean magnitude to 0.11 (vs.\ 0.12). The effect depends on the policy form: occurrence falls under \scen{NDY} (86\% to 40\%) and bounded decline (71\% and 58\% to 38\% and 40\%), but rises slightly under symmetric bounded deviation (71\% and 79\% to 75\% and 85\%). Under volume \scen{NDY} the open-loop plan schedules negatively valued CM-CE harvest equal to 2.5--3.5\% of projected volume on landbase~1 (the inconsistent-variable \textit{filler}); under revenue \scen{NDY} it is exactly zero. On the paired landbases that differ only by the CM-CE ecoclass (1, 3, 5, 7 vs 2, 4, 6, 8), volume-denominated plans are inconsistent in 77/80 vs 51/80 flow-constrained cells, revenue-denominated plans in 58/80 vs 56/80. The negatively valued strata therefore add to the inconsistency through the filler channel, but it persists without them: the objective-gap diagnostic shows 65\% of revenue-denominated replan tails to be strictly suboptimal or infeasible. The revenue-\scen{NDY} plan also front-loads harvests, with a period-1 harvest 34\% above the volume-\scen{NDY} plan on landbase~1 at 4\%.

\textbf{E4: The divergence is robust to alternative implementations of non-declining yield but sensitive to how the constraint is anchored.} Under the within-plan institution, rolling-mean \scen{NDY} is indistinguishable from pointwise \scen{NDY} (occurrence 89\% vs.\ 86\%; magnitude 0.10). Under the realized-history institution, occurrence falls to 69\% and magnitude to 0.072: binding the future planner to what actually happened mitigates the divergence. But the floor then frequently cannot be sustained from the depleted realized state. In 21\% of replan periods (up to 32\% at a 6\% discount rate), the policy must be relaxed, such that the failure to maintain non-declining yield is recorded as infeasibility rather than occurring through implicit abandonment of the previously announced trajectory. Both approaches present plausible models of planning practice. Their contrasting outcomes show that the replanning institution is itself consequential, rather than merely a robustness consideration in the core design.
